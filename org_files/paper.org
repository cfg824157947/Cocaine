*** Single-cell pre-analysis
We first performed rigorous quality control to filter out low-quality cells.
For scRNA-seq, cells with fewer than 200 and more than 1600 detected genes, more than 6% 
mitochondrial transcripts, or exceeding 4000 total counts were removed. 

After filtering, we selected informative features for each modality. 
Highly variable genes (HVGs) were identified from the RNA data using 
<method, e.g. Seurat/Scanpy>, and accessible chromatin peaks were filtered 
to retain regions with at least <C> counts across cells. These steps 
reduced noise and emphasized biologically relevant signals.

Normalization was performed separately for each modality. scRNA-seq data 
were normalized using <log-normalization/SCTransform>, while scATAC-seq 
counts were transformed with <TF-IDF/other method>. This ensured that 
differences between modalities were not driven by sequencing depth.

For dimensionality reduction, we computed principal components (PCs) 
using 15 PCs for RNA and ATAC. Harmony was applied to 
the embeddings of each modality to mitigate batch effects across different sample.

To create a joint embedding, the corrected embeddings from RNA and ATAC 
were concatenated. This allowed us to 
capture both transcriptional and chromatin-accessibility variation in a 
shared space. 

Clustering was performed using the Leiden algorithm across a 
range of resolutions, and we selected a resolution that produced a 
biologically meaningful number of clusters (sufficient to distinguish 
major cell types while avoiding over-fragmentation). For visualization, 
we applied UMAP with parameters n_neighbors=10 and min_dist=<G>.

This pre-analysis provided a high-quality joint embedding, suitable for 
downstream identification of cell populations and integrative analysis of 
RNA and ATAC modalities.

* Peak calling
** Outline
- Separate fragment to each cluster
- macs2 recalling
- generate cluster peaks count and union peaks count

** Draft
We aggregated fragments by cluster and called peaks per cluster with MACS2
(--nomodel --shift -100 --extsize 200, q=0.01; hg38). Peak sets from all
clusters were merged to a consensus (“union”) peak set using sorted
concatenation and bedtools merge. Using this consensus, we quantified
accessibility per cell by intersecting fragments with consensus peaks and
grouping counts by (peak, barcode). For QC, we required ≥N cells or ≥M
fragments per cluster before peak calling, and kept peaks observed in at
least K clusters/samples to reduce noise. The resulting (peaks × cells)
sparse matrix was used downstream for joint RNA–ATAC integration.

*** Correlation analysis using Fisher z test
To assess whether correlations were systematically stronger within groups
than between groups, we applied a Fisher z–based test. For each pair of
features, Pearson correlations were computed and annotated according to
whether both belonged to the same preference group (within-group) or to
different groups (between-group). Correlation coefficients were transformed
using the Fisher z-transformation,
\( z = \tfrac{1}{2}\ln\big(\tfrac{1+r}{1-r}\big) \), which stabilizes
variance and approximates normality.

We then calculated the mean Fisher z for within-group and between-group
edges and obtained their difference (\(\mu\)). The standard error of this
difference was estimated as

\( sd = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N-3}\left(\frac{1}{n_{\text{within}}} +
\frac{1}{n_{\text{between}}}\right)} \),

where \(N\) is the number of samples used to compute each correlation, and
\(n_{\text{within}}\), \(n_{\text{between}}\) are the counts of within- and
between-group edges, respectively.

The resulting test statistic was \( Z = \mu / sd \). A one-tailed p-value
was derived as \( p = 1 - \Phi(Z) \), where \(\Phi\) is the cumulative
distribution function of the standard normal distribution. This tests the
hypothesis that within-group correlations are greater than between-group
correlations.

** Draft
For each genetic line, we calculated gene-level log fold change (logFC) between the cocaine (Coc) and sucrose (Suc) groups.
To assess similarity of treatment effects across lines, we computed correlations of logFC values for each pair of lines.
These correlations were grouped into within-group (same preference) and between-group (different preference) categories.
Statistical testing was performed to evaluate whether within-group correlations were significantly higher than between-group correlations.
In this analysis we found that there are no significant differences between the two groups and all them correlation coefficients are small.
This suggests that the treatment effects on gene expression are not similar across lines, regardless of their preference for cocaine or sucrose.

For Gene regulatory network(GRN) analysis, we computed a Differential TF Activity (DTFA) score for each TF in each line.
The DTFA score is a weighted sum of logFC values of its target genes.
Similar to gene-level analysis, we assessed the pairwise correlation of DTFA scores across lines,
separated them into within- and between-group categories, and tested for significant differences.
In the analysis of DTFA score, we found that there are significant differences between the two groups,
indicating that TF activity patterns differ more between preferences than within them.

For significant TFs, we compared DTFA scores between mid-preference and high-preference, for the cell types that we found significant differences in the previous analysis.
Each cell types have their own set of significant TFs.
